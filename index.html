GRANULAR SYNTHESIS

In this method an already existing audio sample is broken down into tiny, microscopic pieces called grains. These grains are re-arranged by overlapping, stacked or randomized to create brand-new textures, tones or ambient soundscapes.

GRANULAR SYNTHESISER

It is either software or hardware capable of performing granular synthesis. It can turn any audio file into an instrument that you can play on a keyboard. While a traditional synthesizer can only generate basic sound waves like sine and square waves, a granular synthesizer uses an audio sample as its engine.

DIFFERENT TYPES OF GRANULAR SYNTHESISERS

  1. Sample based: Grains are pulled from an audio file loaded inside the synthesizer.
  2. Cloud/real time: Grains are pulled from a live input as it is happening; no pre-recorded audio file is used.
  3. Physical/hardware granular units: Dedicated pedals and hardware synthesizers perform the same process outside software.

WHERE A GRANULAR SYNTHESISER IS USED

  1. Ambient and experimental music production.
  2. Sound design for film and games, turning a single recorded sound effect into an evolving texture.
  3. Glitch and IDM-style electronic music.

HOW THE GRANULAR-SYNTHESISER-ENGINE WORKS

Any sound uploaded into the granular synthesizer engine undergoes granular synthesis.

Position: Marks where the grains are in the source audio.

Scan: Moves through the sound so it evolves continuously instead of looping the same fragment forever.

  • Linear: Moves at a constant speed.
  • Sine: Moves like a pendulum.
  • Wander: Moves unpredictably.

Grain: The microsegment of the source sound. Grain size can range from large (400 ms) to small (10 ms).

  • Size: The length of each grain. Large grains sound smoother and more recognizable; small grains can sound granular, glitchy, buzzy or noisy.
  • Density: The number of grains fired per second. Low density produces individual blips; high density creates a thick, continuous blur.
  • Pitch: How high or low each grain sounds, measured in semitones.
  • Shape: Controls each grain's fade curve. Smooth fades symmetrically, Rise swells then cuts off, Fall starts loud and fades away, Noise adds randomness, and TRI/RECT provide simple geometric fades.
  • Spike: Controls the intensity of the selected shape.
  • Reverse: Sets the probability of grains playing backwards: 0% is normal playback and 100% reverses every grain.

FM (FREQUENCY MODULATION)

One oscillating signal modulates the pitch of another.

  • Rate: Controls oscillation speed in Hz.
  • Depth: Sets the pitch range in semitones; at 0 there is no FM effect.

FLUXUS

Fluxus adds randomness to whether a grain plays and how loud it is.

  • Mute: The chance that a grain is silently skipped.
  • Flux: Random volume variation for each grain.

VARIATION

  • Jitter: Adds random positional offset around the scan target.
  • Spray: Adds random timing variation in milliseconds.
  • Pan: Scatters grains across the stereo field.

FILTER

Two independent filters in series shape the tone and colour of the grain stream.

  • LP: Passes lows and cuts highs.
  • HP: Passes highs and cuts lows.
  • BP: Passes a middle band.
  • Notch: Cuts a narrow middle band.
  • Cutoff: Sets the frequency boundary.
  • Res: Magnifies sound at the cutoff boundary.

ENVELOPE (ADSR)

ADSR controls the overall volume from play until stop: Attack reaches full volume, Decay reaches sustain, Sustain holds the level, and Release fades to silence.

SPACE

Reverb mimics acoustic reflections. Delay adds repeating echoes, Time sets the gap, and Feedback controls how much echo feeds into the next echo.
